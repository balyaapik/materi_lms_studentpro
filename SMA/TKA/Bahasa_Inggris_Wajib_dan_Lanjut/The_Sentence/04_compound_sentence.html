1.4 The Compound Sentence

Independent clauses, FANBOYS, semicolon, conjunctive adverbs, comma splice, dan hubungan logis antargagasan.

1. Konsep Inti

Compound sentence menggabungkan dua atau lebih independent clauses yang secara gramatikal dapat berdiri sendiri. Coordination menunjukkan bahwa gagasan-gagasan itu memiliki status sintaktis relatif setara.

Independent Clause + , FANBOYS + Independent Clause

FANBOYS: for (reason), and (addition), nor (negative addition), but/yet (contrast), or (alternative), so (result). Jika coordinator menghubungkan dua independent clauses, comma lazim ditempatkan sebelum conjunction.

Dua independent clauses yang sangat berkaitan juga dapat dihubungkan dengan semicolon: The refill station is free; many students use it. Jika memakai conjunctive adverb, pola formalnya Clause; however, clause atau Clause. However, clause.

RelasiContoh
AdditionReusable bottles reduce waste, and they save money.
ContrastPlastic is convenient, but it creates long-term waste.
ChoiceBring a bottle, or use the refill station.
ResultThe campaign was clear, so more students participated.

2. Kesalahan Umum

✗ Plastic is convenient, it creates long-term waste. (comma splice)

✓ Plastic is convenient, but it creates long-term waste.

✗ The station is free however many students ignore it.

✓ The station is free; however, many students ignore it.

Jangan menggabungkan clause hanya karena keduanya berdekatan secara makna. Pilih coordinator yang benar-benar merepresentasikan addition, contrast, choice, reason, atau result.

3. Bacaan TKA

Green Steps Campaign

Green Steps, an environmental club at a senior high school, wanted to persuade students to reduce single-use plastic. The members had many facts, yet their first campaign poster consisted of a series of short sentences: “Plastic waste is increasing. The school sells bottled drinks. Students want convenience. Reusable bottles are cheaper over time.” Each sentence was correct, but the message sounded disconnected. Their English teacher suggested combining related ideas with compound sentences so that the logical relationships would become clearer. A compound sentence joins two or more independent clauses. Each independent clause can stand alone as a complete sentence, but the clauses are connected because the writer wants readers to process them as one larger idea. The teacher introduced coordinating conjunctions—often remembered as FANBOYS: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, and so. She explained that the conjunction should match the relationship between the clauses. “And” adds similar information, “but” and “yet” signal contrast, “or” presents alternatives, “so” presents a result, and “for” can introduce a reason in a formal style. A comma normally appears before a coordinating conjunction when it joins two full independent clauses. The club revised its poster. “The school sells bottled drinks, but students can bring reusable bottles” expressed contrast. “Reusable bottles reduce waste, and they save money over time” added a parallel benefit. “Bring your own bottle, or use the refill station near the library” offered alternatives, although the first part was an imperative rather than a typical statement. The students also learned about semicolons. Two closely related independent clauses can be joined with a semicolon: “The refill station is free; many students use it every day.” A conjunctive adverb such as “however,” “therefore,” or “moreover” can follow a semicolon, but it usually requires punctuation different from a simple coordinating conjunction. The teacher warned them about two common errors. A comma splice occurs when two independent clauses are joined only by a comma, as in “Plastic is convenient, it creates long-term waste.” A fused sentence occurs when independent clauses are placed together with no punctuation at all. Students corrected these by using a period, a semicolon, or an appropriate coordinating conjunction. They also considered style. If every sentence is compound, writing becomes repetitive. Effective writers vary sentence structures and choose coordination only when ideas have roughly equal grammatical importance. When the campaign began, the club used compound sentences in social-media captions, speeches, and infographic text. The grammar helped them express choices and consequences efficiently: “You can buy another plastic bottle, or you can refill the one you already have.” After two weeks, the number of students using the refill station increased noticeably. The club could not claim that grammar caused the behavioural change, but members agreed that clearer relationships made their message easier to follow. They had learned that a compound sentence is more than two sentences glued together. It is a deliberate way to coordinate complete ideas while showing addition, contrast, choice, reason, or result.
