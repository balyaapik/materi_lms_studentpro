1.11 The Complex Sentence: Present Participle Constructions

-ing participle clauses untuk simultaneous action, reason, manner/result; subject control dan dangling participles.

1. Konsep Inti

Present participle adalah bentuk -ing. Dalam participle clause, bentuk ini dapat mereduksi subordinate clause ketika hubungan dengan main clause cukup jelas. Contoh: Carrying their toolboxes, the students entered the laboratory. Subject yang dipahami dari participle clause biasanya sama dengan subject main clause.

[ Carrying their toolboxes ], the students entered the laboratory. → implied subject = the students

Makna dapat berupa simultaneous/background action (Watching the sensor, Raka adjusted the code), reason (Knowing the battery was weak, the team replaced it), atau result (The wheel slipped, causing the robot to leave the track).

Bahaya utama adalah dangling participle: Walking into the laboratory, the robot was already moving secara gramatikal menyiratkan bahwa robot-lah yang berjalan masuk. Perbaikan: Walking into the laboratory, the students saw the robot moving.

FungsiContoh
SimultaneousStanding beside the robot, the mentor explained the sensor.
ReasonKnowing the battery was weak, the team replaced it.
ResultThe wheel slipped, causing the robot to turn.

✗ Walking into the lab, the robot was already moving.

✓ Walking into the lab, the students saw the robot moving.

Jangan mengubah semua full clauses menjadi participial constructions. Reduction harus meningkatkan concision tanpa mengaburkan subject, tense relationship, atau logical connection.

2. Bacaan TKA

Observing a Robotics Workshop

The robotics club invited younger students to observe a workshop on designing a small line-following robot. The club wanted to write an English article about the activity, but early drafts contained many repetitive clauses: “The students entered the laboratory. They carried toolboxes. They looked at the demonstration.” Their teacher introduced present participle constructions as one way to combine related information when two actions or situations share a clear relationship. A present participle is the -ing form of a verb. In participle clauses, it can introduce supplementary information about time, reason, manner, result, or accompanying action. “Carrying their toolboxes, the students entered the laboratory” presents the carrying action as simultaneous background information. “Watching the sensor carefully, Raka adjusted the program” describes what Raka was doing while he adjusted it. The teacher emphasised that the understood subject of the participle clause normally needs to be the same as the subject of the main clause. This rule prevents dangling participles. “Walking into the laboratory, the robot was already moving” is illogical if the intended walker is a student, because the grammar makes “the robot” appear to be the one walking into the room. A clearer version is “Walking into the laboratory, the students saw that the robot was already moving.” The class learned that compressed structures save words only when the relationship remains unambiguous. Present participle constructions can also suggest reason. “Knowing that the battery was weak, the team replaced it before the test” can be interpreted as “Because the team knew that the battery was weak….” They can describe results as well: “The wheel slipped, causing the robot to leave the track.” In formal writing, such result constructions are common, but students were warned to check whether the result logically follows from the main event. The club revised the article gradually. They did not convert every subordinate clause into an -ing construction. Sometimes a full clause was clearer, especially when subjects differed or when the time relationship needed to be explicit. The teacher described participle constructions as a resource, not a requirement. Good writing chooses between full clauses and reduced structures based on clarity, rhythm, and emphasis. During the workshop, the younger students built simple circuits, tested sensors, and changed the speed of the motors. Standing beside each team, club mentors asked questions rather than giving immediate answers. Observing how the robot reacted to dark and light surfaces, the participants formed hypotheses about the sensor. When a robot failed to turn, students checked both the hardware and the code. The final article was more varied and concise than the first draft. Participial constructions helped the writers connect simultaneous actions and background reasons without repeating the same subjects. More importantly, the students understood the logic behind the form. An -ing phrase does not automatically make writing more advanced. It works well when its implied subject is clear and when the semantic relationship to the main clause is easy to infer. In technical writing, that clarity is especially important because readers must understand exactly who or what performs each action.
